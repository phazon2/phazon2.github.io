Most quoting advice is a number someone else made up. "Don't charge less than £80 an hour." "Don't go by the hour at all." "Charge £300 a day." None of it can be right for you, because the right number depends on what your van, your kit, your insurance and your unpaid hours actually cost — and nobody asking the question knows that figure yet.

This workbook works it out, then prices jobs against it.

It starts with your break-even hourly rate: your real fixed costs for the year, plus what you want to earn, divided by the hours you can honestly bill. That last part is where most people fool themselves. Quoting, travel, invoicing and maintenance are not billable, and they are most of a week.

Then it prices a job. Pick the type of work, enter the measurement, and it returns the price to quote, the hours it will take, the profit left after your costs and your own time, the margin, and the effective hourly rate for that specific job. It ends on a plain verdict: worth doing, thin, or loses money.

There are three multipliers, and they are the part experienced operators will recognise. Access, for the jobs with no parking and a hose run round the back. Condition, for years of neglect. And Unknowns — for the roof you have not been on yet. That is the honest answer to "how do I quote when I cannot see the whole job": you price the uncertainty instead of absorbing it.
