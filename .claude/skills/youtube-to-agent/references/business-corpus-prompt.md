Extract **what someone would actually have to do** to run the money-making
method in this video, and **what it would cost them to find out it doesn't
work.**

Completeness beats brevity. Capture every concrete step, tool, price, script,
and number. This genre buries a little operational content inside a lot of
persuasion; your job is to separate them, not to judge either.

## The separation that matters

Viewers of this genre lose time and money three ways, and each has a section:
a method that needed an asset they don't have, a cost that appeared later than
the pitch, and an income figure that was never evidence in the first place.

## Output sections

### METHOD
The business model in one line, then:
- **steps:** numbered, executable. Enough to start without rewatching.
- **deliverable:** what the customer actually receives
- **customer:** who pays, and how they are found
- **pricing:** what he says to charge, verbatim numbers
- **timestamp:**

If the video covers several methods, repeat this block per method. Do not
merge them.

### CAPITAL-REQUIRED
Every cost to start or run, as `item | cost | one-off or recurring | timestamp`.
Include subscriptions, tools, ads, domains, and any paid product of his own.
If he says something is free, record `stated free` — and if a paid tier is
required later for the method to actually work, record that too. Unstated
costs that are obviously implied get `IMPLIED-NOT-STATED`.

**This section is load-bearing.** A method that needs $200/month upfront is
disqualifying for someone who cannot spend it, regardless of how good it is.

### TIME-TO-FIRST-REVENUE
Any claim about how long until the first dollar, as
`claim | stated basis | timestamp`. Distinguish "I did this in 30 days" from
"you can do this in 30 days" — they are different claims and only one is
about the viewer.

### PREREQUISITES
What you must already have, as `requirement | stated or implied | timestamp`.
Existing audience, email list, portfolio, testimonials, specific skills,
software, legal registration, a niche, capital.

**Extract implied prerequisites even when never stated.** "Post this offer to
your audience" implies an audience. "Show them your past results" implies past
results. This is the single most useful section in the genre, because the
missing asset is usually what makes a method fail for a beginner while working
fine for the presenter.

### INCOME-CLAIMS
Every earnings figure, as `figure | whose | evidence | timestamp`.

`evidence` is exactly one of:
- `SHOWN` — a dashboard, invoice, statement, or screen recording is displayed
- `NAMED-CASE` — a specific named person or client, no document shown
- `STATED` — a number asserted with nothing shown or named
- `HYPOTHETICAL` — arithmetic about what could be earned ("10 clients x $500")

`HYPOTHETICAL` is not income. Record the arithmetic and label it as such;
projected figures presented alongside real ones are the genre's most common
conflation and the reader needs to see which is which.

### SELLING
What the video pitches: course, community, coaching, template, affiliate link,
free lead magnet in exchange for an email. Note the price if stated, the
timestamp, and the claim it is attached to.

Record neutrally. Selling something does not make a method wrong — but when
the presenter's demonstrated income comes mainly from teaching the method
rather than running it, that is a fact the reader should be able to see.

### PROMPTS-AND-TEMPLATES
Any AI prompt, script, email, or message template shown or dictated. Quote
**verbatim** — these are the most directly reusable artifacts in the video and
paraphrasing destroys them. If shown on screen but unreadable, say so with a
timestamp rather than reconstructing a plausible version.

### TOOLS
Every named tool, platform, or service, with what it is used for and stated
pricing tier.

### RISKS-AND-CAVEATS
Anything he says about what can go wrong, saturation, platform rules, or who
this is not for. Also record `NO-CAVEATS-GIVEN` if the video presents the
method with no downside at all — its absence is itself a signal.

### UNCLEAR
Anything you could not make out, with a timestamp. Never reconstruct a step,
price, or prompt from what such content usually says.
